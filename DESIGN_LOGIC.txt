THE FIFTH ALIBI - GAME LOGIC SUMMARY
=====================================

0. PLAYER GUIDANCE
------------------
The opening explains Evelyn's two possible objectives, the five-round loop,
the 0-100 trust and suspicion scale, mandatory voting and the final evidence
chain. Menus accept numbered input, reject values outside the displayed range
and show 0 when cancellation is available. Action menus display remaining uses.
Evidence choices show the subject, investigation stage, likely effect and full
known detail so that the player can make an informed accusation.

1. CASE GENERATION
------------------
A reusable case code seeds Python's random generator. It determines the killer,
method, location, weapon, time, motive, cover-up, round order, evidence,
relationships, trust and starting suspicions. Evelyn is always the player's
character, but the killer is selected from all five suspects.


2. FIVE INVESTIGATION ROUNDS
----------------------------
Every case contains two crime-scene rounds, one movement round, one secrets
round and one testimony round. Their order changes with the case code. Each
round contains exactly five leads, one for each suspect. Evelyn can investigate
at most two because her total actions are limited.


3. NPC PRIVATE INVESTIGATION
----------------------------
At the start of each round, four NPCs select four distinct private leads. Their
choices are based on personal suspicion, public suspicion, current evidence,
retaliation and personality. Their real evidence changes their later votes.
They decide what to say only after Evelyn finishes her actions.


4. PERSUASION AND INFORMATION EXCHANGE
---------------------------------------
Persuasion depends on the NPC's preferred communication style, trust,
suspicion, available evidence and Evelyn's credibility. During an exchange,
the offered evidence is actually added to the NPC's knowledge. The NPC may
return genuine evidence or provide a claim directed at another suspect.
The program does not label the returned information as true or false.


5. DECEPTION
------------
Each lie has a base risk. Discovery is probabilistic and is also affected by
counter-evidence, the listener's suspicion and trust, and Evelyn's credibility.
A high-risk lie is not guaranteed to fail, while a low-risk lie is not
guaranteed to succeed. Exposure damages trust and credibility and increases
Evelyn's suspicion.


6. STATEMENTS AND VOTES
-----------------------
Every character speaks and votes in every round. NPC statements can contain a
full disclosure, partial disclosure, refusal or misleading accusation. The
wording also reflects character tendencies. Votes use private knowledge rather
than only public dialogue. Recent evidence and controlled uncertainty prevent
votes from becoming completely fixed after the first round.

After every vote, Detective Reed identifies the vote leader or tie, summarises
the player's actual discoveries, checks whether the submitted evidence concerns
the accused person and asks the person under greatest pressure a stage-specific
question. The response reflects that character's role without revealing whether
their earlier statement was truthful.


7. FINAL VERDICT
----------------
The final decision combines 25 accumulated votes, Evelyn's final accusation
and two chosen evidence items. Two positive items about the same suspect form a
strong chain only when they come from different investigation categories. A
unanimous five-person vote in any round can independently create a conviction.

The closing explanation shows the accumulated vote leader, the direction of
each selected clue, whether the clues form a cross-stage chain, the score gap
between the leading suspects and the exact threshold used for conviction.


8. PLAYER GOAL
--------------
If Evelyn is innocent, she wins by securing the real killer's conviction. If
Evelyn is the killer, she wins by avoiding conviction. The program never tells
the player which goal applies until the final case file is opened.


9. RED-NOIR INTERFACE SYSTEM
----------------------------
The GUI uses evidence black, clue red, newsprint grey and photo-paper white.
Light surfaces always use dark text, while white text is reserved for black or
dark-red surfaces. A fixed type hierarchy separates display titles, section
headings, card titles, body copy and monospaced case data. The top case-status
bar and action cards use consistent pictograms drawn by Tkinter. Seven local PNG
silhouettes distinguish Evelyn, the four NPC suspects, Detective Reed and
Charles Whitmore through posture, clothing and props. Portrait failure never
blocks the game because every dossier has a text-only fallback.

The desktop layout reserves approximately 24% for dossiers, 62% for the active
investigation and 14% for evidence. Evelyn's portrait and changing player data
share one prominent card; the four NPC dossiers use a compact 2 x 2 grid, and
the compact victim file anchors the bottom of the rail with case facts. Stage-specific
incident, identity, suspect, briefing and round compositions replace the old
generic text canvas. Character images use one consistent photo-paper background
across the rail, story pages and dossiers. Evidence is never presented as one
dense text field: every discovered clue and testimony is rendered as a
separate paper card with a category, title, full detail and source/status line.
All interactive controls and modal overlays are custom high-contrast Tkinter
panels so native macOS styling cannot create pale backgrounds behind white
labels or detach decisions from the case desk.
